Ongoing Research Fall 2026

Emotion Recognition on Audiovisual Data across Languages and Emotions in SAMSEMO

  • Emotion recognition
  • Multimodal
  • Multilingual

Overview

This thesis evaluates several multimodal foundation models on SAMSEMO, a multilingual audiovisual emotion-recognition dataset with more than 23,000 video scenes across English, German, Polish, Spanish, and Korean. I evaluate the models directly on the original audio evidence, without supplied transcripts, and analyze performance at the model × language × emotion level. The goal is to understand where models fail: which emotions they confuse, whether those confusions differ by language, and whether the same weaknesses appear across models.

Motivation

Reliable emotion recognition is relevant to applications where AI systems interact directly with people, including conversational assistants, accessibility technologies, healthcare, and mental-health applications. A model may perform well overall while consistently failing on particular emotions or languages, so aggregate accuracy alone can hide important weaknesses. I am especially interested in whether low-frequency emotions collapse into more common or similar categories: for example, whether fear is interpreted as sadness or disgust as anger, and, where sample sizes allow, whether apparent language-specific differences persist after accounting for genre composition.
