Ongoing Research Fall 2026

Emotion Recognition on Audiovisual Data across Languages and Emotions in SAMSEMO

  • Emotion recognition
  • Multimodal
  • Multilingual

Motivation

Multimodal foundation models are increasingly capable of processing audio and video directly, creating new opportunities for emotion recognition without relying on manually supplied transcripts or modality-specific classifiers. However, their ability to recognize emotions consistently across languages remains poorly understood. This thesis evaluates several general-purpose multimodal foundation models on SAMSEMO, a multilingual audiovisual emotion-recognition dataset containing more than 23,000 video scenes across English, German, Polish, Spanish, and Korean, with manually collected emotion annotations and metadata.

Rather than developing a new emotion-recognition architecture, this work focuses on evaluating the capabilities and failure modes of existing models when they are given the original audiovisual evidence without supplied transcripts. Performance will be analyzed at the model × language × emotion level to determine which emotions are most difficult to recognize, which emotions are systematically confused with one another, and whether these failure patterns are shared across models or specific to particular model families. Particular attention will be paid to low-frequency emotion classes and to whether errors such as fear being interpreted as sadness or disgust as anger vary across languages.

Because SAMSEMO’s language subsets differ in genre and source composition, observed cross-lingual disparities may partly reflect dataset composition rather than language alone. Where sample sizes permit, this thesis will therefore examine whether major differences persist within comparable genre subsets. More broadly, the goal is to identify where contemporary multimodal foundation models fail in multilingual emotion recognition and to clarify whether those failures are driven by model limitations, class imbalance, language-specific behavior, dataset composition, or some combination of these factors.
