Ongoing Research Summer 2026

Challenges in building AI evaluation tools for mental health

  • LLM evaluation
  • Human-LLM interaction
  • Mental health
  • AI safety

Abstract

Large language models are increasingly used for mental-health support, yet the field still lacks reliable methods for determining whether these systems are safe for vulnerable users. This paper examines the barriers preventing mental-health AI evaluation from producing evidence that is transparent and useful for governance. We draw on interviews with 20 benchmark developers, clinicians, lived-experience advocates, and policy researchers to identify recurring challenges in the design, maintenance, and use of mental-health safety evaluations. Across interviews, we find that progress is constrained by misaligned incentives, siloed evaluation tools, limited access to real-world interaction data, and uncertainty about how existing benchmark results should inform regulation. We also find that mental health is inherently pluralistic: definitions of harm vary across populations and clinical perspectives, making a universal safety standard both unrealistic and undesirable. We argue that the field should prioritize building a shared evaluation infrastructure with transparent comparative frameworks and sustained interdisciplinary collaboration so that different definitions of safety can be meaningfully compared rather than reduced to isolated benchmark scores.
